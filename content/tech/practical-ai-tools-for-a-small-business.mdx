I build Closepath and I write this site from a laptop in Las Vegas. The AI tools that have earned a spot are boring. A first draft. A summary of a document I already have the right to use. A formula I then check by hand. A transcript of a meeting I was in.

## The draft I do not send

I asked a chat tool for a Closepath blurb. It added a feature the book does not have. I deleted that sentence and checked the price against the page, $9.99 a month after 7 days, because the model does not know what I charge.

That is the use. A rough email, a product description, a job post. You still read it. The tool does not know your promises.

## Summary, spreadsheet, transcript

Paste a long document you are allowed to use and ask for the dates and the open questions. Then go back to the original. A summary is a map. It is a bad contract.

For a spreadsheet, I ask for the formula and I test it on a row I can do in my head. If the tool "cleans" the file, I keep my copy.

A transcript helps after a call where the other person agreed to be recorded, or after a meeting of my own. Names and dollar amounts are where it slips. I read it before anyone else does.

Dates do not belong in a chat log. [Closepath](/products/closepath) is the book I sell agents for that, a list they control. A transcript will not ping you when an inspection is Thursday.

## What stays out of the box

Passwords, bank logins, full card numbers, unpublished numbers, and anything a client handed me under a duty to keep quiet. If the settings let me turn off training on my chats, I do that before I paste anything I would hate to see quoted. If I cannot tell what the company keeps, I do not paste it.

I pay for one tool at a time. Visible price, a way to export or delete, and a business plan if customer text will go in. One real task for a week. If it does not save me time on that task, I cancel.
