The demo is staged. I still almost bought one last spring because the sample email sounded like something I would send. The sample was a prompt.

Before the card, I read the vendor's own pages. A review site that gets paid when I sign up is a bad place to learn how to cancel.

## The price after the intro

I write down the monthly number when the trial ends, and what costs extra: seats, documents, minutes. If the price is "talk to sales," I decide whether I want that kind of vendor. On this site the services have a number on the page. [Services](/services). That is the standard I am holding other people to.

## What they keep, and who can see a connected inbox

How long do they store prompts, and can I delete them? Does a paid plan train on my chats while the free plan does? I save the policy page the day I subscribe. It will change.

Connecting email or a help desk means those messages can reach their model. I connect the smallest account that proves the tool is worth it. I do not connect a bank login to a writing product.

## Cancel, then one real job

I find the cancel control before I subscribe. I have paid for a tool where cancel was three screens under billing and then a chat that tried to keep me. That friction is part of the price. I also check whether the trial turns into a charge on its own.

Then I give it work I already do. Last week's notes. A real product description. A formula. I count the time I spend fixing the output. The everyday uses are in [practical AI tools for a small business](/tech/practical-ai-tools-for-a-small-business).

Export once before I rely on it. No export, no copy.

This is a buying check, not a security audit. If the tool will hold client secrets, I ask someone who knows my industry's rules before I turn it on. I am not that someone.
