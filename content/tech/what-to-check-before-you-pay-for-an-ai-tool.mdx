The demo is the best minute the product will have. Before you put a card down, check the vendor's own pages. A review site that earns a commission for the signup is a poor place to learn the cancellation rule.

## Price after the trial

Write down the monthly price when the intro ends, and what costs extra: seats, documents, minutes. If you cannot find the number without a sales meeting, decide whether you want that kind of purchase. For a comparison, Anzen posts the price of each service on the page. See [Services](/services).

## What they keep

What does the tool store, for how long, and can you delete it? Does the vendor use your prompts to train a model? A business plan often says no. A free personal plan often says yes. Read the current policy, and save a copy of the page on the day you subscribe. Policies change.

## Who can see a connected account

If you connect email, files, or a help desk, assume those messages can be sent to the vendor's model. Connect the smallest account that proves the value. Do not connect a bank account to a writing tool.

## How you cancel

Find the cancel control before you subscribe. If the only path is a chat that tries to keep you, treat that friction as part of the price. Check whether a trial turns into a paid plan on its own.

## One real task

Give it a job you already do. Last week's messy notes, a real product description, a formula you need. Count the time you spend fixing the output. A perfect demo prompt is not that test. The everyday uses are in [practical AI tools for a small business](/tech/practical-ai-tools-for-a-small-business).

## A way out

Export your work once before you rely on the tool. If there is no export, you do not have a copy.

None of this is a security audit. It is a buying check. If the tool will hold client secrets, ask someone who knows your industry's rules before you turn it on.
