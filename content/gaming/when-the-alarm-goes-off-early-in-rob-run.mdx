The alarm goes off and there is still loot in the building. That is the moment this game is actually about. Not the gunfight. Whether you stay and finish the room or take what you already bagged and leave.

The official help is pretty clear about how a clean job is supposed to go. Disable the alarms. Keep the staff intimidated so they do not raise one. Once it is already going, police and other criminals can come for you. I treat the call as simple. Once the loot on you is worth more than what is left to grab, I go. The rest is a bonus I only chase if the street still feels quiet.

The loot is usually in safes, cabinets, deposit boxes, and other containers, not just crates sitting on the floor. You can still compare what is already in the bag to what is left to open.

I have not found a player count on the HUD. As of the November 20 patch, Rob Run can show which servers your friends are already on, but that is the server list, not how many people are in the building with you. The community wikis do not document a mid-heist lobby count either. You have to listen. Gunfire close by, cars stacking up, players crossing your path. Quiet, normal, or packed. That is the whole census.

Hotspots get more of that traffic. A corner store on a dead street is not the same as a marked building everyone farms. If you have been jumped at the same place three runs in a row, believe that, not a feeling that this time will be different.

Wanted heat is the other clock. The Rob Run wiki says it goes up when you kill civilians and caps at 3, when you are Most Wanted and the whole city can see you on the map. If you stay quiet and do not kill civilians, it should stay off your back, which is why I try to leave before I get there. The same wiki says one civilian kill is one point, but it also notes that number is not confirmed in the game files. Those pages were last updated November 11 and 13, so I still want to recheck the meter in the current build. Official help does not mention the meter at all. What official docs do say is that putting on the mask makes you a criminal in Looty Bay, and you only clear that by leaving the city.

Jail time is a different clock, and it is not ticking up in the room with the bag. Officially, every seven days the Federal Raid wipes plots, bases, weapons, and cash and turns that wealth into jail time and authority. The more you have accrued when the wipe hits, the longer the sentence. The wiki fills in the rate official posts leave out: one year for every $500,000 of property, up to 40 years from one wipe, which it says is $20 million of property. Paying off Lenny's debt, not just carrying it, adds 10 years once. That wipe page is from November 13. A November 17 official patch then added day and general limits on jail time, without publishing the numbers, so the cap may have changed. The wiki also lists a 10,000-year overall cap, but even there that figure is only a player report. Until I see the current caps in game, I am not using jail math as the reason to leave mid-heist. The reason to leave is the bag itself. If you die in Looty Bay as a criminal, the bag is gone. If you surrender, the bag is confiscated and you keep the gear you have equipped. A bribe from money already in the bank can save the bag, but only if the cop takes it.

The exit still has to happen. Get to the vehicle, then actually unload. Officially you stash cash only at Lenny's, stash stolen goods at your base (which other players can raid), or sell at the dollar-sign van or the Sales Center and take the cash off the counter right away, because it can be stolen or confiscated if you leave it there. Drop-off points get camped. Other players can lockpick a trunk. A packed street makes all of that slower and more likely to go wrong. If the room is already loud, I would rather leave with a half bag than drop the whole run outside the door.
