The message is short. You have been selected. The grant is guaranteed. Pay a fee today so they can release it.

I delete those. A real program does not find you on Tuesday and demand a wire on Tuesday night.

## What a real one looks like

Rules on an official site. You apply. A person or a panel says no to a lot of applicants. If money moves, it moves from the agency. Not from a stranger who wants your bank login.

[Grants.gov](https://www.grants.gov/) and [SBA.gov](https://www.sba.gov/) are .gov for a reason. Type them. Do not tap the link in the text.

## I stop reading when I see this

They contacted you first about a grant you never applied for.

They guarantee approval, or they say the funds are "reserved."

They want money up front to release, insure, or process an award you do not have. A government office does not collect that fee in gift cards, crypto, or a wire to a person.

They want a percentage of the award before you hold an award letter.

The site copies a seal and lives on a .com. Close it.

They tell you not to call the agency, your bank, or the [Nevada SBDC](https://nevadasbdc.org/).

A deadline on a public notice is normal. "Pay us before midnight or the grant dies" is not.

## Then what

Do not pay. Do not send a Social Security number or a photo of your ID to a sender you did not look up. Report the impersonation at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/). The Nevada Attorney General takes these too: [ag.nv.gov](https://ag.nv.gov/).
