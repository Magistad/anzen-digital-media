A grant is money you do not pay back, if you actually receive it. Most small businesses never receive one. The programs that exist are competitive, they have rules, and the funder decides. Anyone who says a grant is guaranteed is not describing how grants work.

## Start on official sites

[Grants.gov](https://www.grants.gov/) lists federal grant programs. Read the eligibility section before you read the award size. Many federal grants are for nonprofits, schools, tribes, or state agencies, not for a for-profit shop. If the eligibility line does not include a business like yours, it is not your grant.

[SBA.gov](https://www.sba.gov/) explains small-business funding in plain language, including loans. A loan is not a grant. Do not let a page blur the two. If you have to pay it back, it is not a grant, even if the interest is low.

Your state economic development office, your city, and a local Small Business Development Center often know about smaller programs that never show up in a national ad. Search the office name plus the word grant, and open the result on a government site.

## Write down four facts

For each program, copy four facts from the official page:

- Who can apply.
- What the money can be used for.
- The deadline.
- Whether they require a match, which means money you put in yourself.

If the deadline has passed, it is not a current lead. If the page does not name the agency, find the agency yourself before you trust the page. Programs change. A deadline you saved last month may have moved. The official page wins.
