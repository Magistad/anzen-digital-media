Someone asked me if a name was free because the .com was $12. I told him to close the registrar tab. I am Javier Segura. I run Anzen Digital Media LLC in Las Vegas. I am not a lawyer, and I do not tell people to file.

A trademark search is a pass through records that already exist. It is not a blessing.

## The search I actually open

The U.S. Patent and Trademark Office keeps a public search at [uspto.gov/trademarks/search](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search). I type the exact words first. Then I split them. Then I try the misspelling a tired customer would use. If the name is two words, I search each word alone. "And" versus "&" has burned people. So has dropping a vowel.

The USPTO's own primer on what a trademark is, and what it is not, is here: [trademarks/basics](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics). Read that before you pay anyone, including me, to "check availability."

## The goods line is the part people skip

A record is tied to what the business sells. A taco shop and a software tool can share a word because a customer would not confuse the two. The record lists those goods in numbered classes. If the class is close to what you do, open the whole file. Do not stop at the bold name.

A dead record is a clue. It is not proof the owner took the name off the truck.

Federal registration is also not the whole market. States keep registers. People build rights by using a name in commerce, and no database shows every use. If a search does not say what it left out, it is a brochure.
