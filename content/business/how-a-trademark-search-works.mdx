A trademark search is a look through names and logos that are already on record, before you print a name on a truck or file an application. It is research. It is not a legal opinion, and it is not a promise that you can register the name.

## Start with the free federal search

The United States Patent and Trademark Office publishes a trademark search at [uspto.gov/trademarks/search](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search). Look up the exact words you want to use. Then search the words separately, and search obvious misspellings. A mark that drops a vowel, or that swaps "and" for "&", can still sit close to yours.

Read the goods and services, not just the name. Trademark records are tied to what the business sells. A restaurant and a software company can sometimes use similar words because a customer would not think they come from the same firm. The record lists those goods in numbered classes. If the class and the description are close to what you do, slow down and read the whole record.

A dead record is useful, and it is not the same thing as a live registration. Dead does not always mean the owner has stopped using the name in the market.

## The federal register is not the whole market

A business can use a name without a federal registration. States keep their own registers. People also build rights by using a name in commerce, and a database search will not show all of that use. A careful search says what it looked at and what it did not look at.

## What a useful report contains

When you pay someone to prepare a search report, ask for three things: the marks they found, a link to each official record, and a plain description of how close those marks look to yours. That package is informational. It does not tell you to file, and it does not tell you the name is clear. A trademark attorney is the person who can advise you on filing.
