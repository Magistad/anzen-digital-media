If you have a Nevada limited-liability company, the state expects two things every year: an annual list of who runs the company, and a renewal of the state business license. Both go to the Nevada Secretary of State. They are not a tax return, and they are not an income statement. The list is a roster. The license is the state's business license.

This is general information about published rules. It is not legal advice about your entity. Confirm the date and the fee in your own account before you pay the state.

## When it is due

The annual list is due on or before the last day of the anniversary month. For a Nevada LLC, that month is the month the state formed the company. A company formed on any day in March files by March 31. The business license renewal rides along with the list. You can see the file date in the state's business search and on the stamped formation papers.

Do not treat a reminder email as the deadline. Missing a notice does not excuse a late filing. Put the last day of the anniversary month on your own calendar.

Filing too early can miss the year you meant to cover. Nevada law says a list filed more than 90 days before it is due can be treated as an amended list for the prior year. If you want to file far ahead, read NRS 86.263 or ask the Secretary of State before you do. The statute is on the Nevada Legislature site. The Secretary of State's forms and fees are at [nvsos.gov](https://www.nvsos.gov/).

## What a typical LLC pays the state

The annual list fee published for a Nevada LLC is $150. The state business license renewal published with that filing is $200. Together that is $350, paid to the State of Nevada, not to a helper. Other entity types can owe a different license fee, so a corporation should not assume the LLC total. Late filing can add penalties, and leaving the filing undone can put the company in default. The figure in your portal is the one that counts.

## Where you file

File in your own account on ORION, the Secretary of State's business portal, at [orion.nv.gov](https://orion.nv.gov/). ORION replaced the older SilverFlume portal. If you already had a SilverFlume login, try that same login on ORION. Have the entity name, the Nevada entity number if you have it, and the names and addresses of the managers or managing members. You pay the state fee in that portal.
